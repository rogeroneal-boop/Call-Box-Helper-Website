How to Make Sure UPS, FedEx and Amazon Can Always Reach You

If you live in an apartment or gated community, getting reliable deliveries takes a little more effort than just entering your address. Here's a practical guide to making sure every carrier — Amazon, UPS, FedEx, USPS, and others — can always get through to you.

First — understand why deliveries fail at gated communities

Most delivery failures at apartments and gated communities come down to one thing: the gate. If your building uses a call box system, every delivery requires you to answer a phone call and press a key to open the gate. Miss the call — even by 30 seconds — and the driver marks it as attempted and leaves.

Understanding this is the first step. The tips below help with each carrier specifically, but the root cause is almost always the same: the gate.

Per-carrier tips

📦 Amazon

Amazon has the most robust delivery preference system of any carrier. Go to Your Account → Addresses → Edit and add detailed delivery instructions. Be specific: include your unit number, building entrance, and any relevant details about how to reach you.

For call box buildings, note that delivery instructions alone won't open the gate — a driver still needs you to answer. But clear instructions help drivers know which call box panel to use and which unit to dial.

Amazon Hub Locker — if missed deliveries are a recurring problem, consider having packages sent to a nearby Amazon Hub Locker instead. You pick up on your schedule with no gate issues.

Pro tip: Amazon drivers use a separate app that shows your delivery notes. Clear, specific notes are more likely to be read than vague ones.

🚚 UPS

Create a free UPS My Choice account at ups.com. This gives you the ability to:

  • Get delivery alerts before the driver arrives
  • Reschedule deliveries to a time you'll be available
  • Redirect packages to a UPS Access Point (pickup location)
  • Authorize delivery release without a signature
  • Add delivery instructions to your address

UPS My Choice is free and one of the most useful tools for managing deliveries. The delivery alert feature alone can save you from missed deliveries — you'll get a notification when the driver is nearby so you can be ready to answer your call box.

Note: Delivery alerts give you advance warning but don't guarantee you'll catch the call — drivers can arrive earlier or later than estimated.

📫 FedEx

Sign up for FedEx Delivery Manager at fedex.com. Similar to UPS My Choice, it lets you:

  • Get real-time delivery notifications
  • Request delivery to a FedEx location for pickup
  • Sign for packages remotely
  • Add specific delivery instructions
  • Request a specific delivery window (premium feature)

FedEx also has a network of pickup locations — Walgreens, Dollar General, and FedEx Office locations — where packages can be redirected if gate access is consistently a problem.

✉️ USPS

USPS has the most limited delivery customization options of the major carriers. You can use Informed Delivery (informeddelivery.usps.com) to get daily email previews of incoming mail and packages, which at least gives you advance notice.

For packages that require a signature or gate access, USPS will leave a pickup notice and hold the package at your local post office for 15 days. You can also set up Package Intercept to redirect packages before delivery.

Tip: USPS carriers on regular routes often learn building access patterns over time. If the same carrier delivers to your building regularly, they may already know your call box number.

🛒 Other carriers (DoorDash, Instacart, etc.)

Food delivery and grocery delivery apps typically have a delivery notes field at checkout. Always include your unit number and any gate instructions. For call box buildings, add your phone number directly in the notes so drivers can call you directly rather than using the call box.

Most food delivery drivers will call or text if they can't get through — unlike parcel carriers who are on tighter time constraints.

The problem with all of these workarounds

Every tip above requires you to do something on each individual platform, update it whenever it changes, and hope drivers actually read and follow your instructions. And none of them solve the fundamental problem — if your building has a call box, someone still has to answer it.

Delivery notifications help you be ready, but you still have to answer your phone in a narrow window. Pickup lockers and alternative locations are inconvenient. Instructions help but aren't guaranteed to work.

The pattern: Every carrier-specific workaround is a band-aid. They reduce missed deliveries but don't eliminate them. The only complete solution is to make the gate open automatically — so no driver from any carrier ever gets stuck outside.
