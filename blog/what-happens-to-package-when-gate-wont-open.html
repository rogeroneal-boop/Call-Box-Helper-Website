What Happens to a Package When the Gate Won't Open

You get a notification — "Delivery attempted." But you were home. What actually happened to your package? Where did it go? And how many more times will this happen before it gets returned to the sender? Here's the full story.

The moment the driver gives up

When a delivery driver arrives at your gate and your call box doesn't get answered, they typically wait 20-30 seconds before moving on. After that window closes, a specific chain of events kicks off — one that determines whether you get your package tomorrow, next week, or never.

What happens step by step

📵

Call box goes unanswered

The driver dials your unit number. Your phone rings but you don't answer — silent mode, Do Not Disturb, or you simply didn't hear it. After 20-30 seconds the driver marks the stop and moves on.

📱

You get the "delivery attempted" notification

Within minutes you receive a push notification or email saying delivery was attempted. At this point the package is back in the driver's vehicle, continuing on the rest of their route.

🚚

Package returns to the facility

At the end of the driver's shift, all undelivered packages return to the local distribution facility or warehouse. Your package gets scanned back in and queued for another attempt.

📅

Rescheduled for the next business day

Most carriers will automatically schedule another delivery attempt for the next business day. You may receive a new estimated delivery notification — or you may not, depending on the carrier.

🔁

Second attempt — same problem

The next driver arrives at your gate. If nothing has changed on your end, the same thing happens. Another missed call, another failed delivery, another "attempted" notification.

⚠️

Final attempt — then held or returned

Most carriers make 1-3 delivery attempts before taking further action. After the final failed attempt, the package is either held at a local facility for pickup or returned to the sender.

What each carrier does after failed attempts

Amazon

Amazon typically makes up to 3 delivery attempts. After that, the package may be redirected to an Amazon Hub Locker or pickup location nearby. If no pickup option is available or the package isn't claimed, it gets returned to Amazon and a refund is issued — but you lose the item and have to reorder.

UPS

UPS makes up to 3 delivery attempts on consecutive business days. After the final attempt, the package is held at a UPS Access Point or UPS Customer Center for 5 business days. If not picked up within that window, it's returned to the sender.

FedEx

FedEx typically makes 3 delivery attempts. After the final attempt, the package is held at a FedEx location for 5 business days before being returned. FedEx Delivery Manager users can redirect packages or request holds proactively.

USPS

USPS leaves a pickup notice after a failed attempt and holds the package at your local post office for 15 days. If not picked up, it's returned to sender. USPS typically makes fewer reattempts than private carriers.

The worst case scenario: Some drivers, rather than returning packages to the vehicle, leave them outside the gate entrance. This is especially common with smaller packages that don't require a signature. Packages left outside an unsecured gate are frequently stolen — and recovering them is difficult since the package shows as "delivered."

Why this keeps happening

If you've had multiple failed deliveries at your gate, the pattern won't stop on its own. Each attempt follows the same process — driver arrives, call box dials your phone, you don't answer, package goes back. The cycle repeats until you either happen to answer or the package gets returned.

The frustrating reality is that you can be home the entire time and still miss the delivery — because the call box called while your phone was on silent, during a meeting, or from a number you didn't recognize.
